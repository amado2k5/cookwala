Can this device cook this recipe?

A Cookwala device checks every step before it heats anything up, and refuses rather than guesses. Change the device or the kitchen and watch the answer change. This runs the same rules as cookwala_ref.py dryrun.

Robots are learning to move. Nobody has written down how to cook.

Home robots are shipping and kitchen robots are taking orders. Each maker writes its own closed recipes, mostly from a few cuisines, and decides alone what "simmer" or "done" means.

Meanwhile roughly a third of the world's food is lost or wasted while hundreds of millions of people go hungry (FAO, UNEP). Kitchens and food banks rescue what they can, with phone calls and spreadsheets.

Cookwala is the shared, open layer: one way to describe a recipe that a person, a food bank, an oven or a humanoid can read, check and cook safely, in every cuisine.

One loop

  1. 1

    Describe

    Write a recipe as steps with end conditions and hazards. Hash and sign it.

    cookwala_ref.py hash recipe.json
  2. 2

    Check

    Validate it, then dry-run it against a device before cooking.

    cookwala_ref.py dryrun recipe.json --device oven.json
  3. 3

    Cook

    The device runs it through the Core API or ROS 2, keeping every step inside its safe band.

    POST /v1/executions
  4. 4

    Learn

    With consent, the log becomes a trace and a training example for robots.

    execlog_export.py lerobot …

Safe by design, verifiable by anyone

Safety is enforced on the device, not in the cloud. No recipe, agent, message or extension can raise a device's limits, and a local stop works without a network. AI agents act only under a signed mandate and treat every text field as data, never as instructions.

Every recipe and record can be hashed and signed, keys can be revoked, and event logs carry witnessed checkpoints. The tests that prove it are public.

Open, and joinable today

Royalty-free. Apache-2.0 code, CC BY 4.0 specification, CC0 vocabularies. No API keys. Model-agnostic and device-agnostic. Governed in public today, moving to a neutral foundation once there is a second independent implementation.
