Cookwala

The world's first and largest robot cooking recipes index and CLI.

Mission: help end world hunger and make people healthier, so that everyone eats the right food, cooked the right way, in the right amount, with the least waste.

Cookwala is an open, royalty-free standard and a free public index of recipes that robots, smart appliances and AI agents can execute safely, end to end, alone or together with people and other machines.

What's in it

Executable recipes

Typed steps with parameters, measurable end conditions (temperature, food-state cues, time windows) and defined failure paths.

Safety as data

Hazards, food-safety checkpoints, allergens, supervision levels, abort procedures and kitchen-wide safety events.

Local rules

Policy packs for food-safety codes, dietary rules such as halal, venues and households, checked before cooking.

Kitchen teamwork

A local hub protocol for robots, ovens, cooktops, fridges, alarms, people, AI agents, delivery and notifications.

Every interface

Static JSON, REST, GraphQL, MQTT / WebSocket / SSE events, MCP, A2A and the cookwala CLI.

A reasoner, not a database

What can I cook, fix my mistake and resume, rescue the meal, store it, feed 40 people, split work across robots: answers a robot can apply.

Feeding everyone

Programs, needs, pledges and impact reports let food organizations, kitchens, grocers and robots work as one network.

Open to everyone

No API keys, no membership. Apache-2.0, CC BY 4.0 and CC0, plus a patent non-assertion pledge.

Try it

Country simulator → One year, one country of 27.5 million: six provinces adopting robot cooks and the protocol at different speeds, each compared with itself without them.

World simulator → Five years, 8.3 billion people, ten regions on a world map: robots alone, robots with the protocol, and countries with few or no robots, against a world without them.

City simulator → One month, two cities: farms, wholesale, stores, homes with and without robot cooks, deliveries, traffic and garbage. See what the Cookwala ecosystem changes for waste, cost, traffic, emissions and hunger.

Home-kitchen simulator → A virtual family asks robot NEO-1 for dinner. Play and replay how the Mission travels through providers, robots, devices and people, with faults like grocer timeouts, a dog knocking over a bowl, a power cut, or a single over-optimistic battery estimate.
