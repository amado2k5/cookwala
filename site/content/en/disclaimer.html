Disclaimer

Cookwala helps people and machines refuse unsafe cooking steps. It cannot make a device, a meal or a decision safe on its own. Last updated 11 October 2026.

Kitchen devices, robots and AI agents

  • Cookwala is a draft open standard and a set of reference tools. It is not a safety standard recognised by any regulator, and it does not replace the product-safety, electrical, appliance, machinery, robotics or AI requirements that apply to a device (for example CSA and UL standards in North America, IEC 60335 and CE marking with the EU Machinery Regulation and AI Act in Europe, or FCC and ISED rules).
  • Passing the conformance tests, a dry run in the playground or the CLI, a simulator result or a Cookwala certification record shows only that the published rules were applied to the inputs given. It does not show that a device is safe, that its sensors are accurate, or that a recipe will turn out safe to eat.
  • Safe operating bands, envelopes and rule packs are derived from published guidance and are awaiting professional review. They may be incomplete or wrong for a given device, ingredient, altitude or situation.
  • The service is read-only and can never start cooking. Anyone who connects a device or an AI agent to Cookwala data is responsible for the device, the agent, the mandates they grant, supervision, and every action taken.
  • Never leave cooking unattended where a device, a recipe or local rules require supervision. Keep a working local stop, smoke detection and a fire extinguisher suited to kitchen fires.

Food safety, allergies, health and diet

Recipes come mostly from a family archive and credited sources; V0 recipes are described, not machine-verified, and none has been tested in a professional test kitchen. Allergen, diet (vegetarian, vegan, halal, kosher, gluten-free, lactose-free and others) and diabetic labels are inferred from ingredient names and published claims: they are screens, not guarantees, not religious rulings and not certifications. Nutrition and cost figures are estimates. Nothing here is medical, nutritional, allergy or dietary advice. Read the label of every product, follow your local food-safety authority, and ask a doctor, allergist or registered dietitian about allergies and conditions.
