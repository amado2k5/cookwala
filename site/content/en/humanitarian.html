From surplus to a plate, safely, with the phones you already have

The Humanitarian Profile is a small, strict data standard for food banks, community kitchens, school-meal and relief programs. Offer, claim, hand over with a temperature check, serve, count. It works by SMS and spreadsheet, with no personal data, no robots and no internet required. Robots and apps can use the same documents later.

The path, end to end

  1. Offer. A store, farm, caterer or kitchen sends what it has: kilograms, storage, temperature, date mark, allergens. OFFER 36KG YOGURT C 4C UB0511 or FARM 120KG TOMATO A BB0411.

  2. Claim. A food bank, kitchen or program claims all or part, within a priority tier the program publishes. CLAIM A7K ALL.

  3. Handover with a cold-chain check. At every change of custody a trained person records the temperature and the kilograms accepted or rejected with a reason. A temperature-controlled line without a reading is not recorded; hot food below 60 °C is refused. HAND A7K 36 T4.6, or HAND A7K 30 REJ 6 PACK T4.6 to reject part of it.

  4. Kitchen. A community, school, disaster or robot kitchen cooks; a production run can record cooking, hot-holding and cooling temperatures (experimental fleet profile).

  5. Distribution. Meals and people served at a site on a day, as counts only; any count under ten is "fewer than ten". DIST 410 MEALS 410 PEOPLE 96KG; per-meal nutrients with MENU.

  6. Impact. A summary computed from the documents: kilograms rescued, meals, cost per meal, time to claim, safety findings and incidents, each with its method and the number of documents behind it. The nutrition pass rate is self-reported unless nutrients come from linked recipes and portion masses.

The SMS walkthrough

Type the messages a donor and a food bank would send, or press the buttons. The gateway in your browser turns them into the profile's documents, applies the cold-chain checks (missing reading, chilled above 5 °C, hot below 60 °C, a chilled-class item declared ambient), and computes the summary. Nothing is sent anywhere.

No personal data, by design

    
  • Every party is an organization: a did:web domain, a GS1 location number or a registry id. People appear only as roles ("trained staff").
  • Counts, never records: people served by group, any group under ten reported as "fewer than ten".
  • Sites are premises or administrative areas, never a household.
  • Notes are 280 characters, scanned for phone numbers and ids before storing.
  • Retention declared and enforced; optional hash-only audit log; in-country hosting where required.

    
People are partners in this work, described by role and situation. No faces, no poverty imagery, no saviour language, on this site or in any program material we produce.

  

  

    
Food safety and nutrition rules, reviewable

    
Four draft rule packs derived from public WHO, Codex and Sphere guidance: cold chain, the two-hour rule for cooked food out of temperature control, date marks, allergens (cooling stages and reheating are recorded in a kitchen's production run, not in these packs); sodium, free sugars, fats, fruit and vegetables; care rules for children under five, pregnancy, older adults and immunocompromised people; school-meal patterns; sodium reduction.

    
Every pack says who reviewed it, by profession. None is reviewed yet; that is the first thing we ask of dietitians and food-safety officers. The two-hour review template · What we never claim

  






  

    
Running a pilot honestly

    

      
Before
Hypotheses and thresholds pre-registered; a four-week baseline of current practice; a data-responsibility review against ICRC and OCHA guidance; ethics approval where required; rule packs adapted by the program's food-safety lead.

      
During
Twelve weeks, at least two sites and five donors, a comparison site where possible; recording time per 100 kg measured; kill criteria: any attributable safety incident, double the recording time, or the partner's request.

      
After
The summary computed from the documents, an independent evaluator who reads the raw records, and publication whatever the result shows, with a "what went wrong" section. The partner is named only with its written agreement.

What we don't know yet
  • Whether a program rescues more with the profile than with its current method.
  • How much recording time the SMS grammar costs a volunteer in practice.
  • Whether the rule packs fit national law anywhere; adaptation is the program's.

  
What went wrong
  • Nothing recorded yet: no deployment has happened.






  

    
Interoperability

    
CSV templates carry HXL hashtags; items carry GS1 GTINs and sites carry GLNs or OCHA P-codes; distributions map to DHIS2 aggregates; beneficiary systems are never touched (aggregates only cross). The organizations behind these standards are not partners; their formats are used so that programs' existing tools can read ours. Section 9 of the profile.
