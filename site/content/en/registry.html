Find and publish, under names that prove who published

A registry holds pointers, not content: what exists, who published it, which exact version, and its hash. Names are proven by DNS or a code-host account; versions are exact; withdrawn entries stay as tombstones. Anyone may run a registry. This one lists only what exists in the repository today, and says so.

Publish

  1. Validate locally with the same checks the registry runs.

    cookwala validate
cookwala hash my-collection/recipe.cookwala.json

  2. Prove your namespace once: a DNS TXT record cookwala-verify=<token> on your domain, a /.well-known/cookwala-verify file, or a GitHub or GitLab OIDC token from a CI job in your account.

  3. Publish the entry.

    curl -X POST https://cookwala.ai/v1/registry/publish \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer $COOKWALA_PUBLISH_TOKEN" -H "Content-Type: application/json" -d @entry.json

    next The publish and validate endpoints are specified (registry.openapi.yaml) and come with the registry service. Today entries are added by pull request to site/v1/registry.json.

Rules

  • Names: <reverse-dns-namespace>/<name>, lower case, 3 to 200 characters.
  • Versions exact (1.4.2); ranges and latest rejected.
  • Every version records its artifact's sha256; recipes also carry their own document hash.
  • Lifecycle: active → deprecated → recalled (also in the recall feed) → deleted (tombstone). Names are never reused.
