# Like bees

The founder asked for a system that works the way nature works: bees without a queen giving
orders, slime moulds finding the shortest paths, immune systems remembering threats. No
central command, and yet harmony and recovery.

It is a seductive picture and a dangerous one. Nature's decentralised systems are also wasteful,
slow to change, and indifferent to the individual bee. A food system that behaved like a hive
would let a region starve while the colony as a whole did well. So the question is not whether
to imitate nature but which of its mechanisms carry over to a system that must also be fair,
auditable and kind.

Cookwala borrows five and refuses two.

**Marks, not orders.** Ants leave pheromone trails; they do not send instructions. Cookwala's
network is made of signed feeds that nodes read and republish: recalls, incidents, registry
changes, keys. Nothing is pushed into a kitchen. A kitchen pulls what it trusts, when it is
online, and keeps working when it is not.

**Evaporation.** Trails fade. Every fact in a household context has a validity; every registry
entry has a status; every recall an issue time. Nothing is trusted for being old.

**Verify the source, not the messenger.** A bee trusts the dance, not the bee. A relayed recall
verifies against the issuer's key, never the relay's. A mirror that edits what it carries
breaks the signature.

**Local rules, global order.** A device enforces its own limits; a hub keeps its own trust
list; a program runs its own registry. The order that emerges is the order of many local
refusals, not of one controller.

**Immunity.** Anonymous incident reports become antibodies: a near miss in one kitchen becomes
a rule in all of them.

The two refusals. **No quorum where a person should decide.** Bees choose a nest by quorum;
a household chooses dinner by asking the person who will eat it. The Mission profile keeps
quorum decisions for narrow machine questions and marks them experimental. **No lean system
without reserves.** The macro promise of planned cooking is that only what is needed gets
grown and moved. Nature keeps fat. A food system tuned perfectly to demand is one shock away
from an empty shelf, as the pandemic showed. The supply-signal design says reserves are part
of the plan, not waste.

What nature cannot teach is fairness. A hive has no notion that the far field deserves
foragers. Cookwala's answer is governance with seats for the people the system is for, and
signals that are public and identical for every reader. That is not a natural mechanism. It is
a political one, and it is the part that will decide whether this works.
