# Messaging: what Cookwala says, and how

**Status:** 2026-10-04. Written before the pages. Every page follows this document.

## 1. The one sentence

**Cookwala is the open standard for cooking safely: people, kitchens and robots.**

Arabic: **كوكوالا هو المعيار المفتوح للطهي الآمن: للناس والمطابخ والروبوتات.**

## 2. The triad, used everywhere

A Cookwala recipe says three things a machine can check:

1. **What to make.** Recipes as steps a machine can plan.
2. **When it's done.** Temperatures, food-state cues and times it can measure.
3. **What must never happen.** Safety limits the device enforces on itself.

Arabic: **ماذا نطبخ. متى ينضج. ما الذي لا يجوز أن يحدث أبدًا.**

## 3. The mission, told honestly

- **Mission:** help end hunger, make people healthier, and put robots to work for people.
- **How we say it:** "Cookwala helps end hunger by…" followed by a mechanism (less waste,
  safer rescue, cheaper meals, coordination), never "Cookwala ends hunger".
- **The caveat, once per page where the mission appears:** hunger has many causes: poverty,
  conflict, climate, prices, policy. Cookwala's part is real and partial.

## 4. Three horizons, with restraint

| Horizon | Say | Do not say |
|---|---|---|
| Now (to 2028) | Safer devices that refuse rather than guess; recipes that work across machines; food banks rescuing more with phones and spreadsheets; agents that can't be tricked; open tools you can run today | "widely adopted", "proven" |
| Next (2028 to 2036) | Home and commercial robots that cook every cuisine; a consented dataset that keeps recipes alive; new roles; less waste; cheaper, healthier meals; a standard regulators point to | "robots in every home", numbers of robots |
| Later (2036 and beyond) | Cooking as infrastructure: nutritious food for anyone, anywhere, including disaster zones and people who can't cook; less waste and lower emissions; human time returned; machines that inherit culinary heritage instead of erasing it | dates, counts, "end hunger" |

The reader discovers the scale by following the mechanisms; we do not announce it.

## 5. Writing rules

- Plain words, short sentences. One idea per sentence.
- No buzzwords: not revolutionary, disruptive, AI-powered, world-class, first and largest.
- Every number is **measured**, **modelled** or **assumed**, with its source, visible next to
  the number.
- **Now / next / later** for anything that does not exist yet. Never imply a service exists.
- No partner, user, pilot, quote, logo or endorsement that does not exist. Organizations
  appear as sources or as "who we want to work with", labelled.
- People are described by role and situation, never by deficit. No "the vulnerable" as a
  noun; "children under five", "people who cannot cook for themselves".
- Safety is a number with a unit, never the word "safe" alone.
- Refusal is good news. Say "the device refuses before anything heats up" with pride.
- Say what we do not know and what went wrong, on every impact and roadmap page.
- Code in code blocks; expected output after every command.
- Arabic is a first-class version, not a translation afterthought: same structure, same
  honesty, right-to-left layout, Arabic numerals as the reader expects (Western digits are
  acceptable in technical content).

## 6. Vocabulary

| Use | Instead of |
|---|---|
| recipe, step, done, safe band, limit, refuse, check, verify, consent | instruction set, AI brain, smart, autonomous |
| device, executor, hub | the robot (unless a robot is meant) |
| people who cannot cook for themselves | the elderly, the disabled |
| food bank, community kitchen, program | beneficiaries, recipients |
| measured, modelled, assumed | estimated, projected (without a label) |
| now, next, later | coming soon, roadmap item (without a horizon) |

## 7. Proof we may show today

Counted from the repository at build time (`/v1/stats.json`): operations with physical
definitions, conformance vectors, safety limits, agent-safety tests, humanitarian rules,
schemas, published recipes, languages, simulators. Each shows its kind. Nothing else is a
number unless a source is cited.

## 8. Calls to action by verb

try (dry run, simulator) · read (quickstart, Core, whitepaper) · build (SDK, hub, MCP) ·
publish (recipe, device, pack) · pilot (humanitarian) · review (rule pack, envelopes) ·
teach (lesson kit) · legislate (model language) · partner and invest (investors page) ·
contribute (RFC, translation, vectors).

## 9. Headline bank

- The open standard for cooking safely.
- What to make. When it's done. What must never happen.
- Devices that refuse rather than guess.
- Food that would be thrown away, reaching a plate, safely, by SMS.
- Every cuisine, with the cook's name on it.
- Safety is local. Consent is explicit. Records can be checked.
- Robots are learning to move. Nobody had written down how to cook.

## 10. What every page carries

A status chip (Core normative, draft profile, experimental, planned), the one sentence in
the footer, licences per layer, a link to the critiques, and a "what we don't know yet" block
on any page that states an outcome.
